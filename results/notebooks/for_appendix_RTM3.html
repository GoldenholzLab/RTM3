RTM3 scientific appendix

This appendix reports every simulated condition, selection diagnostics, correction comparisons, and the fixed-cohort sensitivity analysis. These additional analyses were performed during manuscript revision, not prespecified. All counts are simulated.

RTM denotes regression to the mean. False-alarm rate (FAR) is the expected number of false alarms per day.

The analyses ask how detector performance and the enrollment rule change apparent improvement when there is no treatment effect. Observed RTM is the proportion meeting a strict trajectory criterion. Median percentage change (MPC) measures the median percentage reduction from baseline. These are different outcomes and can move in different directions.

The fixed perfect-detector cohort is hypothetical and completely unrealistic as an enrollment strategy for a trial whose eligibility is determined by the imperfect detector being evaluated.

Some retained participants would have failed that detector’s entry criteria. The comparison tests how the result depends on changing enrollment. This qualification concerns this particular enrollment strategy; it does not imply that every fixed-cohort trial design is unrealistic or that the diagnostic comparison is mathematically invalid.

Shared participant characteristics and reproducibility

Each of 100,000 potential participants has a 60-day baseline, a 90-day test period, and no treatment effect. A separate 1,080-day realization estimates effective long-term frequency. A simulated month is 30 days throughout. The two realizations share the sampled monthly rate, cluster status and settings, and cycle frequencies and amplitudes. Their random count realizations differ. The long-term diary is not a continuation of the trial diary, and shared cycle parameters do not imply a shared daily intensity trajectory.

At one sample per day, the unchanged CHOCOLATES source cannot represent 12-hour or 24-hour within-day cycles. Its configured weekly component has zero amplitude. Longer cycles, when drawn, remain possible. The daily study therefore does not reproduce the simulator’s full within-day rhythmic behavior. Empirical diaries used to develop CHOCOLATES can contain reporting error; treating generated events as latent biological truth is a modeling assumption.

The base seed is 20260813, the nested detection seed is 20260814, and the nested false-alarm seed is 20260815. Lower-sensitivity detections are contained in higher-sensitivity realizations. Higher false-alarm conditions add independent Poisson increments. Every eligibility/correction comparison uses the same latent participants and the same detector realization at that condition. Intervals quantify Monte Carlo sampling uncertainty conditional on this model, not uncertainty about its assumptions or real devices.

Observations and monthly correction

For participant ii and day dd, let SidS_{id} be latent seizures, ss sensitivity, and λ\lambda the false-alarm rate in alarms/day:

Did∣Sid∼Binomial(Sid,s),Fid∼Poisson(λ),Yid=Did+Fid.D_{id}\mid S_{id}\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(S_{id},s),\quad F_{id}\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda),\quad Y_{id}=D_{id}+F_{id}.

False alarms are independent of latent counts. Let YimY_{im} be the observed total in a 30-day month. Expected-alarm correction gives

Cim=max(0,Yim−30λ).C_{im}=\max(0,Y_{im}-30\lambda).

Here DidD_{id} counts detected latent seizures, FidF_{id} counts false alarms, and YidY_{id} counts all detector events. The corrected monthly total CimC_{im} subtracts the expected number of alarms from observed monthly total YimY_{im} and floors negative values at zero.

Before flooring, subtracting the known expectation leaves mean-zero false-alarm error in the unselected population. It does not identify individual alarms. Flooring and selection can change the residual error among selected participants. This correction targets sensitivity-scaled latent counts, but it is not an unbiased estimator after flooring.

Eligibility and additive thresholds

The main rule uses only the two baseline months:

Ei=𝟏{Ci1≥3,Ci2≥3,(Ci1+Ci2)/2≥4}.E_i=\mathbf1\{C_{i1}\ge3,\ C_{i2}\ge3,\ (C_{i1}+C_{i2})/2\ge4\}.

The additive alternative selects on raw observed counts:

Eiadd=𝟏{Yi1≥3+30λ,Yi2≥3+30λ,(Yi1+Yi2)/2≥4+30λ}.E_i^{\mathrm{add}}=\mathbf1\{Y_{i1}\ge3+30\lambda,\ Y_{i2}\ge3+30\lambda,\ (Y_{i1}+Y_{i2})/2\ge4+30\lambda\}.

The indicator 𝟏{⋅}\mathbf1\{\cdot\} equals one when every condition inside the braces holds and zero otherwise. Thus Ei=1E_i=1 means that participant ii is eligible; EiaddE_i^{\mathrm{add}} is the same decision expressed on the raw-count scale. All baseline inequalities include equality.

The equivalent two-month total threshold is 8+60λ8+60\lambda, retaining the separate monthly minimum. Because each eligible corrected month is at least three, neither is on the zero floor. Subtracting 30λ30\lambda from each raw month therefore yields exactly the primary inequalities. Thus Eiadd=EiE_i^{\mathrm{add}}=E_i for every participant. This is an algebraic identity, not only a simulation result. The notebook verifies mask and outcome equality at all 11 false-alarm rates. Both analyses retain corrected outcome counts. Raising raw thresholds changes how the rule is expressed but does not improve precision or remove random false-alarm error.

Effective long-term reference and strict endpoint

Let LiL_i be the integer seizure total in the separate 36-month realization:

fi*=Li/36,ri=sfi*.f_i^*=L_i/36,\qquad r_i=s f_i^*.

The effective long-term frequency differs from the sampled rate parameter because the realization includes count variability, clustering, and configured cycles. For mean monthly measured baseline BiB_i and test frequency TiT_i, observed RTM is

Ri=𝟏{Bi>ri,Bi−ri>|Ti−ri|}.R_i=\mathbf1\{B_i>r_i,\quad B_i-r_i>|T_i-r_i|\}.

Here fi*f_i^* is effective latent seizures per month and rir_i is its sensitivity-scaled reference. The measured frequencies BiB_i and TiT_i are the averages over two baseline months and three test months, respectively. The indicator RiR_i requires baseline to exceed the reference and test to be strictly closer to it. Its prevalence is the number with Ri=1R_i=1 divided by all selected participants.

Both comparisons are strict. Equal distances do not count. The canonical grid uses an exact common integer scale for the two-month baseline, three-month test, rational sensitivity, and 36-month reference. Retaining LiL_i prevents floating-point errors at true ties. Genuinely noninteger inputs outside that grid use a documented relative equality tolerance of 10−1010^{-10}.

The separate uncorrected-outcome comparison uses its observation-scale reference:

riraw=sfi*+30λ.r_i^{\mathrm{raw}}=s f_i^*+30\lambda.

That comparison uses raw counts for eligibility and outcomes with the original fixed count thresholds. It differs from the additive-threshold analysis, which retains corrected outcomes. Comparing raw false-alarm-contaminated outcomes with sfi*s f_i^* would mix count scales and is not done here. Exact oracle removal subtracts realized labeled false alarms and uses the latent-count reference. It is invariant across false-alarm conditions by construction.

The primary aggregate observed-RTM analysis describes trajectories. It does not identify an isolated participant-level RTM Type 3 component or divide overall RTM into additive causal parts. The additional paired analysis below compares measured and latent classifications within each detector-selected cohort and reports an operational measurement-only category, with separate reverse discordance.

Percentage change and denominators

For positive measured baseline:

PCi=100(1−Ti/Bi),MPC=mediani:Ei=1,Bi>0(PCi).PC_i=100(1-T_i/B_i),\qquad\mathrm{MPC}=\operatorname{median}_{i:E_i=1,\ B_i>0}(PC_i).

Here PCiPC_i is a participant’s percentage reduction from measured baseline, so positive values mean fewer measured seizures during test. MPC is the median of those defined reductions; it is not the percentage of participants showing observed RTM. For fixed membership, the selection indicator in this median refers to perfect-detector enrollment, while BiB_i and TiT_i still come from the detector condition being evaluated.

All main-analysis participants have positive baselines. Fixed-cohort participants can have zero measured baseline after detector performance changes. Their percentage change is undefined, regardless of test count. Tables report the full selected count, zero-baseline count, and positive-baseline MPC denominator. No pseudocount is added. Observed RTM always uses the full selected denominator, including zero-baseline participants, who do not meet its criterion.

MPC intervals are nonparametric binomial-order-statistic 95% intervals. CSVs also contain Wilson intervals for selection and observed RTM. No hypothesis tests or between-condition confidence intervals are reported. Conditions share participants, so pointwise intervals are not independent-comparison intervals.

Fixed versus reselected cohorts

Reselection applies eligibility to the condition-specific detector baseline counts. It models that detector determining enrollment. The fixed cohort consists of the 35,393 patients who met eligibility under perfect detection, s=1s=1 and λ=0\lambda=0. Their identities are retained regardless of whether they would qualify under an imperfect detector. Both strategies share the latent seizure histories and the exact same detector realizations at each condition. Membership is fixed, not successful eligibility under each detector.

This is a hypothetical diagnostic comparison of dependence on changing enrollment. It is not a realistic prediction of enrollment in a detector-selected trial, an additive causal decomposition, or an isolated RTM Type 3 estimate. At 10% sensitivity, 33,213 retained participants fail the current detector’s criteria; at one false alarm/day, 11,351 fail them. These failures are distinct from the 6,182 and 538 zero measured baselines excluded from MPC, respectively. Observed RTM retains all 35,393 patients in its denominator.

Fixed-cohort outcomes describe changes in measured trajectories without changing initial enrollment. However, MPC remains conditional on positive measured baseline, and that subset can vary. The selected count is constant, but its MPC denominator need not be. This limits interpretation of low-sensitivity fixed-cohort MPC.

The 25-day rule

Let Li(Z)L_i(Z) be the longest consecutive zero-day run in the 60-day baseline. The interval sensitivity adds Li(Z)≤25L_i(Z)\le25 to monthly criteria. The original daily correction is

Zid=max(0,Yid−round(λ)).Z_{id}=\max(0,Y_{id}-\operatorname{round}(\lambda)).

Here ZidZ_{id} is the daily count used only to determine zero-day runs; Li(Z)L_i(Z) is the longest such run. The maximum run is inclusive at 25 days. Monthly enrollment criteria and outcome counts continue to use CimC_{im}.

NumPy nearest-even rounding subtracts zero from zero through 0.5/day and one from 0.6 through 1.0/day. This discontinuity changes zero-day classification without identifying false alarms. Alternatives use observed days, Zid=YidZ_{id}=Y_{id}, or oracle detected-seizure days, Zid=DidZ_{id}=D_{id}. Only the interval component changes; monthly outcomes retain expected-alarm correction. Oracle intervals and exact oracle alarm removal are separate diagnostics.

The 30-day months adapt clinical criteria rather than reproducing four-week trial windows exactly. Dropping the interval rule removes the need to assign corrected events to days, but it does not eliminate monthly noise or flooring.

Participant-level measurement-only RTM in detector-selected cohorts

For this paired analysis, we operationally define RTM Type 3 as measured RTM that is absent when the same participant is classified using latent counts. We use measurement-only RTM as the descriptive label for this category.

This additional analysis asks which participants meet the measured strict RTM criterion but do not meet the latent strict RTM criterion. It uses participants who actually qualify under each detector’s primary monthly criteria: each corrected baseline month has at least three events and their two-month mean is at least four. It applies no 25-day rule and no perfect-detector enrollment filter. Participants who fail latent eligibility or have zero true baseline remain in the paired analysis; strict RTM is defined for them even when a true-count percentage change would be undefined.

The five distinct conditions are sensitivity 0.1, 0.5, and 1.0 at FAR zero, and FAR 0, 0.1, and 1.0 per day at sensitivity 1.0. Perfect detection is the shared control. These are subsets of two separate sweeps, not a factorial design. Within each condition, measured and latent classifications use the same selected identities and latent histories. Across conditions, enrollment can change, so differences combine selection and measurement.

Measured counts retain the primary pipeline: subtract the known expected alarms from each 30-day monthly count and floor the result at zero. The measured reference is sensitivity times the participant’s independent 36-month latent frequency estimate. Latent classification uses true monthly counts and the same estimate without sensitivity scaling. That finite-history reference is an estimate, not a known infinite-horizon mean. The paired contrast therefore covers detection noise, false alarms, expected-alarm correction, and flooring together.

Both classifications use the strict RTM definition above: baseline must exceed the relevant reference, and test must be strictly closer to it. Baseline and test frequencies are the corresponding two-month and three-month mean counts. Equal baseline and test distances do not count as RTM. The production indicator receives the original integer 36-month reference total through its long_term_total keyword. An independent audit uses equivalent strict integer cross-products of baseline totals, test totals, reference totals, and rational sensitivity, avoiding floating-point ambiguity.

For the paired table, “both” means measured and latent RTM; “measurement-only” means measured RTM with latent RTM false; “latent-only” means latent RTM with measured RTM false; “neither” means both false. These four counts sum to the full selected denominator in each condition. The primary measurement-only fraction divides measurement-only cases by all selected participants. The secondary fraction divides the same numerator by participants with measured RTM. Reverse discordance divides latent-only cases by all selected participants. The measured-minus-latent prevalence difference subtracts latent-only cases from measurement-only cases, divides by all selected participants, and is reported in percentage points. It is a net difference, not the gross measurement-only fraction. None of these measures is MPC.

Both measurement-only fractions have 95% Wilson intervals calculated with the unchanged canonical helper. These describe conditional Monte Carlo/binomial uncertainty under this simulation, not real-world clinical uncertainty or uncertainty about model assumptions. Shared histories make the detector conditions dependent; the pointwise intervals are not confidence intervals for between-condition differences. No new significance tests are performed.

Under perfect detection, the measured and latent inputs are identical, so zero discordance is guaranteed. The Wilson interval at that control is a mechanical summary; its upper bound does not imply that discordance can occur under perfect detection.

“Measurement-only RTM” is an operational, simulation-defined paired category. It is not exclusive biological attribution or an additive causal decomposition of RTM Type 3. Latent histories are treated as truth under the simulation model; the shared finite reference and detector-selected population also condition the result. The analysis complements the primary aggregate comparisons and the hypothetical fixed-cohort diagnostic without changing their interpretation.

It generated all 100,000 latent participants and replayed every original 0.1-step nested detector condition before retaining these five settings. The long-term total array hash and selected/measured-RTM counts at all 21 released sweep rows matched exactly. The remote software and operating system differ from the released environment; those differences are recorded. The release did not record daily-array hashes, so count agreement does not establish an independently verified byte match to unavailable released daily arrays.

Interpretation

With perfect detection, 35,393 of 100,000 participants were selected, observed RTM was 37.2%, and placebo median percentage change was 14.3%. Under reselection, the corresponding observed-RTM and median-change estimates were 64.4% and 48.1% at 10% sensitivity, and 53.6% and 31.2% at one false alarm/day.

Holding the perfect-detector cohort fixed gave observed RTM of 29.3% and median change of 33.3% at 10% sensitivity, and 34.7% and 10.4% at one false alarm/day. Median change was defined for 29,211 and 34,855 selected participants, respectively; 6,182 and 538 had zero measured baseline. These comparisons describe the influence of allowing cohort membership to change. They are not additive causal components of RTM, and the fixed-cohort median has a performance-dependent valid-baseline denominator.

Moving expected false alarms from the counts to the eligibility thresholds produced exactly identical results. It does not reduce the remaining random false-alarm error.

Retaining the original 25-day rule changed selection by at most 2.28 percentage points, observed RTM by 2.49 points, and MPC by 2.29 points. The main directions remained. Alternative interval rules gave different results because observed alarms and oracle seizure labels define different zero-day runs. The original rule’s larger overall maxima include the sensitivity sweep; the observed-day and oracle-day alternatives were evaluated only on the 11-point FAR sweep. On that common FAR sweep, the observed-day and original rules have equal maximum absolute changes from the main analysis: 0.9571218755 percentage points for observed RTM and 0.8658008658 percentage points for MPC. The oracle-day rule has larger maxima on that same FAR grid. These comparisons concern maxima across conditions, not a uniform ordering at every condition.

Without any count correction or threshold adjustment, FAR one/day selected 100,000 participants, observed RTM was 27.1%, and MPC was 0.0%. This does not establish that uncorrected counts are preferable: alarms allow low-seizure-rate participants to pass fixed raw thresholds and contribute to both the baseline denominator and test counts. The reference now includes expected alarms so that the RTM comparison stays on the observation scale. Exact oracle removal retains the perfect-detector results at every FAR because it removes all realized simulated alarms. It is a diagnostic, not an available correction for unlabeled clinical events.
